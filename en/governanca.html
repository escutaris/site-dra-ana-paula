Governance · Indicators · Decision

When a company measures better, it decides better.

And when it decides better, it turns mental health into culture, leadership and long-term value. This page brings together the indicators and questions that strengthen management.

The bill nobody wants to see

Cost, return and what global data shows.

Mental health at work is no longer just an HR topic. It is a balance sheet line, culture, productivity, leadership and psychosocial risk.

Global cost US$ 1 trillion

In lost productivity every year. The WHO estimates that 12 billion working days are lost annually to depression and anxiety.

Documented return 4.7x

Average return on investment. A Deloitte UK analysis indicates a return of about £4.70 for every £1 invested in mental health and well-being support.

Scale of the problem 15%

Of working-age adults were estimated to be living with a mental disorder in 2019, according to the WHO. The figure reinforces the need for management, prevention and support at work.

Sources: WHO, Mental health at work; Deloitte UK, Mental health and employers 2024.

The gap between intention and governance

The market recognizes the problem. Few companies turn it into decisions.

Although 97% of large companies recognize mental health as a priority, that recognition rarely becomes governance, transparency or an auditable KPI.

13%

CEOs make public commitments on the topic.

Few companies turn internal recognition into a public position from senior leadership.

33%

They train line managers.

Most still do not prepare leaders to recognize suffering, hold difficult conversations and act with preparation.

21%

They use data to shape strategy.

Many companies measure engagement, but few turn that material into consistent decisions about mental health.

10%

They disclose impact KPIs.

Low transparency shows how often intention is communicated without demonstrating real results.

0%

They have the system audited externally.

Among the largest companies assessed, none has a mental health management system audited by a third party, a field still immature in governance.

Mental health at work is not strengthened by intention alone. It is strengthened when the company learns to measure what reveals trust, stress, support, balance and real culture change.

Source of the percentages: CCLA Corporate Mental Health Benchmark Global 100+ 2025, a report based on public information from the companies assessed.

Golden Rules · Questions and Answers

The questions that strengthen management.

Twelve questions for senior leadership to assess real maturity in mental health at work, psychosocial risks, NR-1 and the indicators that turn intention into decisions.

01 How do I know whether my company is creating the safety for people to speak up? +

One of the most important signs is the percentage of employees who feel safe to talk openly with their direct manager about mental health, emotional difficulties and overload. When this figure is low, the company may have formal channels but still lack real trust.

02 What does a good well-being indicator need to capture? +

Measuring overall satisfaction is not enough. More mature companies look at how people feel about work-life balance, perceived well-being and trends over time. This kind of reading helps adapt practices, routines and work models more intelligently.

03 How can you tell whether people know where to get help? +

An essential indicator is internal awareness: how many employees know where to find support, what kind of support exists and how to access it. A company may offer valuable resources and still have low effectiveness if the workforce does not know they exist or does not trust them.

04 Why does measuring perceived stress help you act earlier? +

Because this data allows predictive action. When the company tracks the percentage of people reporting high levels of stress, it can see patterns before the impact turns into sick leave, conflict, a drop in performance or more evident illness.

05 What shows that an initiative is changing behavior, not just discourse? +

Tracking attitudes, uptake and changes in behavior toward healthier habits and lifestyles helps you understand whether the culture is really moving. When there is no concrete change, communication may be strong, but transformation has not yet reached everyday life.

06 What do these indicators reveal to senior leadership? +

They help answer a strategic question: are the resources invested in mental health improving only the company's image, or also its culture, trust and the real experience of work? When properly tracked and disclosed, these indicators strengthen transparency, consistency and decision-making capacity.

07 Which is worth more: measuring a lot or measuring better? +

Measuring better. The value lies in choosing indicators that make sense for the company's reality and make it possible to track trends, gaps and the concrete effects of actions. The goal is not to inflate dashboards, but to support more responsible decisions that are more useful to the operation.

08 What is the question every leader should ask? +

Do the people in my company feel safe, supported and informed enough to ask for help before their suffering gets worse?

09 How does NR-1 connect to mental health governance at work? +

NR-1 (Brazil's occupational risk management standard) requires occupational risks to be identified, assessed and managed within the company's Occupational Risk Management (GRO) and Risk Management Program (PGR). When psychosocial factors enter the conversation, the company needs to move beyond one-off actions and build evidence: where the risk appears, who is exposed, what controls exist and how leadership monitors progress.

10 What is the difference between an emotional support benefit and psychosocial risk management? +

Emotional support benefits help people who are already suffering. Psychosocial risk management also looks at how work is organized: workload, autonomy, fairness, role clarity, relationships, conflicts and leadership practices. A mature company needs both, but should not confuse individual support with prevention at the source.

11 Which mental health indicators can a company track without exposing people? +

Useful indicators can be tracked in aggregate: absenteeism, sick leave, turnover, program uptake, perceived support, psychological safety, workload, leadership quality and use of care channels. The point is not to watch individuals, but to see patterns in order to act responsibly.

12 When does it make sense to bring in external technical support? +

When there is recurring illness, sensitive conflicts, doubts about the link with work, return after sick leave, regulatory pressure or difficulty turning data into decisions. Technical support helps separate opinion from criteria, protect people and guide the company without reducing everything to internal communication.
